Rotary Joint Seal Replacement: A Step-by-Step Guide for Maintenance Teams

📅 May 24, 2026 ⏱ 10 min read 🔧 Intermediate 🌟 Begapunk Engineering
Share: LinkedIn X Facebook WhatsApp

2Disassembly

  1. Mark the rotor orientation with a paint pen or tape. Some Begapunk models have asymmetric inlet/outlet channels. Reversing the rotor 180° swaps the flow paths.
  2. Remove the anti-rotation pin or bracket (if fitted). Note the angle — it must be reinstalled in the same orientation.
  3. Loosen the end cap using the correct wrench size. Do not use adjustable wrenches — they round off the hex flats.
  4. Slide the rotor shaft out of the housing. Support the shaft body, not just the end — the inner seal surfaces are precision-ground and scratch easily.
  5. Extract the old seal by hand or with a seal puller. Never use a screwdriver tip.

3Clean the Housing

This is the most skipped step and the most common cause of post-replacement leakage.

  1. Wipe the seal groove with isopropyl alcohol and a lint-free cloth. Remove all old seal fragments, lubricant residue, and contamination.
  2. Inspect the groove under magnification (or a borescope if the housing is deep). Look for:
    • Scratches parallel to the seal running direction (acceptable if shallow)
    • Scratches perpendicular to the seal path (unacceptable — creates a leak channel)
    • Corrosion pits (common in aluminum housings used with water)

If you find groove damage: Stop. A damaged groove will leak with any seal. Contact Begapunk support with the serial number on the housing body. We can identify the correct replacement housing or recommend a repair sleeve.

4Install the New Seal (Direction Matters)

This is the critical step. Every seal type has a correct orientation, and installing it backwards guarantees immediate failure.

FKM O-Ring Seals

  • Installation: Stretch gently over the rotor shaft and seat into the housing groove.
  • Direction: O-rings are symmetric — no directional concern. But ensure the groove is completely filled; a twisted O-ring leaks immediately.

Glyd Ring (PTFE Composite)

  • Structure: A rectangular PTFE ring with an FKM elastomer energizer embedded in the back.
  • Direction: The PTFE face must face the pressure side (the side where the medium enters). The elastomer energizer faces the low-pressure side.
  • Begapunk identification: Every Glyd Ring seal packet has a directional arrow stamped on the PTFE face. The housing groove also has a corresponding mark.

Common mistake: Installing the Glyd Ring with the elastomer side facing pressure. The elastomer extrudes under pressure, the seal jams, and the joint seizes within hours.

PTFE Skeleton Oil Seal

  • Structure: A metal-cased seal with a PTFE lip and a garter spring for radial tension.
  • Direction: The PTFE lip must face the medium (the side to be sealed). The metal case faces outward.
  • Begapunk identification: The inner lip edge is slightly chamfered. The chamfer always faces the sealed cavity.

Installation technique: Use the installation sleeve to push the seal squarely into the bore. Do not tilt it. A tilted seal creates a gap on one side that leaks under the slightest pressure.

5Reassembly and Torque

  1. Lubricate the seal lip lightly with the medium it will seal (a drop of clean air tool oil for pneumatic joints, clean water for coolant joints). Never use grease on PTFE.
  2. Insert the rotor shaft carefully, aligning the orientation marks from Step 2.
  3. Install the end cap by hand until finger-tight.
  4. Torque the end cap to 25–30 N·m using a calibrated torque wrench.
    • Over-torque (>35 N·m): Strips the housing threads or cracks the cap.
    • Under-torque (<20 N·m): Insufficient seal compression. Leakage under pressure.

Begapunk ships every joint with a torque card in the packaging box. The card lists the correct torque for your specific model and thread size. Keep it in your maintenance manual.

6Post-Installation Test

Do not pressurize to full operating pressure immediately.

  1. Apply 0.2–0.3 MPa test pressure (compressed air or nitrogen).
  2. Check all seal interfaces with soapy water or a leak detector.
  3. Rotate the shaft slowly by hand. Listen for scraping or squealing — both indicate seal misalignment.
  4. If no leak: Gradually increase pressure in 0.2 MPa steps up to operating pressure.
  5. If leak detected: Stop immediately. Disassemble and recheck seal direction and groove cleanliness.

4. Most Common Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Forcing the Seal In with a Hammer

What happens: The impact deforms the seal lip geometry. A deformed lip cannot maintain uniform contact pressure against the shaft. It leaks from day one.

Correct method: Use the installation sleeve. Push evenly with your thumb or the palm of your hand. If the seal resists, check that the bore is clean and that you have the correct seal size.

Mistake 2: Installing Without Cleaning the Groove

What happens: Old seal fragments and debris trapped under the new seal create high spots. The seal cannot seat fully. A 0.05 mm gap is enough to leak at 0.6 MPa.

Correct method: Alcohol wipe the groove three times. Inspect with a flashlight. If you see anything other than clean metal or specified surface finish, clean again.

Mistake 3: Skipping the Low-Pressure Test

What happens: If the seal is backwards or damaged, applying full pressure immediately blows the seal out of the groove or burns the lip.

Correct method: Always start at 0.2–0.3 MPa. Verify no leak. Then step up. This 5-minute test saves a 3-hour rework.

Mistake 4: Reusing the Old Anti-Rotation Bracket Without Checking

What happens: The bracket bore wears over time. A loose bracket allows the housing to rotate incrementally, twisting the inlet hose and applying side load to the seal.

Correct method: Check the bracket bore for oval wear. If the bracket rocks on the housing pin, replace the bracket.

Conclusion

Seal replacement is not "pop the old one out, push the new one in." It is a controlled procedure: depressurize, mark orientation, clean the groove, install the seal in the correct direction, torque to specification, and verify with a stepped pressure test.
