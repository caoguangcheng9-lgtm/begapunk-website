Material & Seal Selection Notes

AL6061 aluminum body: 6061-T6 aluminum alloy with yield strength 276 MPa and hardness 95 HB. The anodized surface provides corrosion resistance for intermittent water and coolant exposure. At 0.39 kg, it keeps rotating inertia manageable on medium rotary tables. Not recommended for continuous abrasive coolant without additional surface hardening — specify hard anodizing (Type III, 25 μm) or stainless steel (304/316) for such environments.

PTFE composite seal: Polytetrafluoroethylene with glass fiber and graphite filler. Self-lubricating, chemically inert, and rated for -200°C to +260°C. The composite structure provides structural integrity while maintaining the low friction coefficient (0.05–0.10 against aluminum) that makes PTFE ideal for rotary seals at 150 RPM. FKM O-ring provides static sealing at the housing interface.

Deep groove ball bearing: Single-row deep groove ball bearing (6202 or equivalent) with steel shields and lithium grease lubrication. The bearing supports radial loads up to 5 N·m and axial loads up to 2 N·m. This is the key difference from plain-bearing joints — it allows side-mounted tooling, off-center fixtures, and cantilevered loads that would cause rapid seal wear in a sleeve-bearing design. Bearing life is rated at ≥8,000 hours under rated load.

Installation Guide

Proper installation extends seal life from 6 months to 12+ months and protects the deep groove ball bearing from premature failure. The most common cause of early bearing wear on BP-2P-30-0001 is side-loading from rigid piping or misaligned flanges. Follow these steps for reliable operation.

1

Verify Flange Compatibility

Confirm your equipment flange dimensions and bolt pattern match the rotary joint flange specification. BP-2P-30-0001 uses a flange mount with specific P.C.D. and hole count. Do not force-mount on a mismatched pattern — drilling new holes in the Ø76 mm aluminum body creates stress risers that propagate under cyclic loading. Download the 2D drawing for exact flange dimensions, bolt circle diameter, hole size, and counterbore depth. Check flatness with a feeler gauge: the mating flange must be flat within 0.05 mm to prevent seal distortion.

2

Apply Flange Gasket and Tighten Bolts

Place the gasket between the flange faces. Tighten bolts evenly in a cross pattern to the specified torque. For M5 screws: 3–4 N·m. Overtightening beyond 5 N·m warps the AL6061 flange face, compressing the PTFE seal unevenly and creating an inter-passage leak path. Use a torque wrench and tighten in three stages: 50%, 75%, then 100% of final torque in a star pattern. This ensures even clamping force across the Ø76 mm flange diameter.

3

Secure Anti-Rotation

If your model includes an anti-rotation tab, secure it to a fixed bracket with an M4 or M5 screw. Never let the stationary body rotate with the shaft — the supply hoses will twist and the seal will experience reverse torque, accelerating wear. The anti-rotation arm should have 2–3 mm of clearance to allow for axial float during thermal expansion. A rigid anti-rotation arrangement that transfers vibration or side load into the housing can increase bearing load and may shorten service life.

4

Connect Supply Lines with Flexible Hose

Use flexible hoses (not rigid pipe) to connect the stationary side to the air supply. Rigid pipe transfers vibration and thermal expansion directly to the joint, stressing the seal and bearing. Install a 40-micron filter upstream to protect the PTFE seal from debris. For water or coolant, add a Y-strainer (60 mesh) to prevent particulate damage. Label the two passages (e.g., "Passage A" and "Passage B") to prevent cross-connection during maintenance. Use M5 push-to-connect fittings with thread sealant — do not overtighten plastic fittings into the aluminum ports.

5

Run-In Procedure

Run at low pressure (0.2 MPa) and low speed (30–50 RPM) for 5 minutes without load. This beds the PTFE seal against the shaft surface and distributes grease within the deep groove bearing. Check for leaks at the flange gasket and seal interfaces. If dry, gradually increase to operating pressure and speed over 10 minutes. Skipping the run-in is the #2 cause of early seal failure on ball-bearing joints — the seal needs time to conform to the shaft surface finish, and the bearing needs initial grease redistribution before full radial load.

Maintenance Schedule

  • Air duty, 8-hour shift: Inspect every 6 months, replace seal every 10–12 months, check bearing noise annually
  • Water or coolant duty: Inspect every 4 months for corrosion and seal wear, replace seal every 8–10 months
  • High-cycle indexing tables (1,000+ cycles/day): Replace seal every 6–8 months, inspect bearing every 6 months
  • Keep spare seal kits and one spare bearing at the workstation — a 20-minute seal and bearing change beats a half-day trip to stores

Common Mistakes

3 Mistakes That Destroy BP-2P-30-0001

Mistake 1: Exceeding the 5 N·m radial load limit

The #1 cause of BP-2P-30-0001 bearing failure is side-loading beyond the deep groove bearing rating. A machine builder mounts a 3 kg pneumatic grinder on a 200 mm arm attached to the rotating flange. The resulting radial load is 8 N·m — 60% over the bearing limit. Within 4 months, the bearing develops play, the seal wobbles, and both passages leak. Rule: Keep radial loads ≤5 N·m. For heavier side loads, specify a tapered roller bearing custom design or support the tooling with a separate bearing block.

Mistake 2: Using rigid pipe and ignoring flange flatness

A builder connects the stationary side with rigid steel tubing and mounts the joint on a hand-machined flange with 0.15 mm warp. The rigid pipe prevents the joint from self-aligning, and the warped flange creates a 0.15 mm gap at one edge. After 2 weeks, air leaks at 0.3 MPa from the high side of the flange. The builder replaces the gasket twice before realizing the flange needs resurfacing. Rule: Always use flexible hose (minimum 8 mm bend radius) and ensure the mating flange is flat within 0.05 mm. Check with a feeler gauge before installation.

Mistake 3: Skipping the run-in and immediately applying radial load

A maintenance team installs BP-2P-30-0001 on a welding positioner, skips the 5-minute run-in, and immediately starts production with a 4 N·m side load. The PTFE seal has not bedded into the shaft surface, and the bearing grease has not redistributed. Within 6 weeks, the seal leaks and the bearing growls. The team replaces both components, but the same thing happens because they skip the run-in every time. Rule: Run at 0.2 MPa and 30–50 RPM for 5 minutes without load before full operation. This beds the seal and distributes bearing grease, extending life from 6 months to 12+ months.
