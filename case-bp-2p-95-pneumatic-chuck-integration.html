Engineering Challenge

The assembly needed separate compressed-air paths between a stationary supply and rotating chuck components without allowing the machine-side hoses to turn with the chuck.

  • Route the pneumatic circuits through the chuck's rotating structure.
  • Fit the rotary union and hose connections within the available axial and radial envelope.
  • Maintain a clear relationship between stationary plumbing and rotating components.

Integration Approach

In the photographed arrangement, the rotary union is positioned on the chuck's axis of rotation. Stationary-side air lines connect to the union body, while the rotating-side connection supplies the chuck mechanism.

Before a design is released, the machine builder should verify concentricity, port orientation, hose bend radius, mounting dimensions and interference clearance against the approved drawing for the ordered configuration.

These photographs document workshop assembly details. Their captions describe visible components and do not turn the images into performance claims.

What this case demonstrates

  • BP-2P-95-0001 has been mechanically integrated into a real chuck structure.
  • The visible hose and mounting arrangement fits within the photographed assembly space.
  • The installation provides a documented starting point for pneumatic chuck engineering review.

What the photographs do not prove

  • Final service life or maintenance interval.
  • Leakage performance under every operating condition.
  • Undisclosed pressure, speed or duty-cycle capability.
  • Final machine delivery, customer acceptance or long-term production operation.
