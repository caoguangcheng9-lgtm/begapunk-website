# Manufacturing & Quality — Anodizing Evidence Record

- Date: 2026-08-08
- Public page: `manufacturing-quality.html` and the `de/`, `ja/`, `ru/` localized editions
- Scope: rotor hard anodizing, stator-housing color anodizing, low-speed O-ring running-surface context, and one photographed coating-thickness reading
- Source authority: owner-provided manufacturing photographs and owner-confirmed construction details

## Approved factual scope

1. The pictured construction uses a hard-anodized aluminum rotor and a color-anodized stator housing.
2. Hard anodizing is described as supporting surface hardness, wear protection, and corrosion protection. For low-speed dynamic O-ring sealing, it may help reduce direct aluminum-surface scratching and groove-wear risk.
3. The stator housing may use a technically available anodized color selected for machine styling or identification, subject to physical-sample approval and normal variation from alloy, preparation, geometry, and process lot.
4. The coating-thickness instrument in the supplied photograph visibly reads `51.7 μm` at the pictured point on one rotor sample.
5. The assembly and batch photographs document visible manufacturing, handling, assembly, and packaging-preparation states only.

## Evidence limits carried into public copy

- `51.7 μm` is a single-part, single-point photographed reading. It is not represented as a whole-part distribution, a batch average, a fixed tolerance, or a specification applying to every model.
- The reading does not by itself prove instrument calibration, wear life, corrosion duration, surface hardness, O-ring life, leakage performance, or test acceptance.
- The photographs do not establish passage count, pressure rating, test result, customer identity, order identity, shipment approval, or in-service performance.
- No MIL Type III compliance, fixed hardness, salt-spray duration, exact RAL/Pantone match, zero wear, maintenance-free operation, or quantified life improvement is claimed.
- Final performance also depends on post-treatment surface roughness, O-ring material, lubrication, media, pressure, speed, alignment, temperature, duty cycle, and contamination.
- Coating build-up must be accounted for in post-treatment dimensions, fits, grooves, ports, and mounting interfaces.
