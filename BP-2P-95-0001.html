Installation Guide

Proper installation is important at the approved 1 MPa maximum pressure. Incorrect alignment, flange preparation, hose loading, or fastener selection can distort the sealing interface and cause leakage. Follow the official drawing and application-specific engineering guidance.

1

Verify Flange Bolt Pattern

Confirm the equipment flange matches the official 2D drawing before machining or assembly. Verify the exact bolt pattern, P.C.D.s, hole diameters, depths, and tolerances shown in the drawing. Do not infer fastener sizes from webpage text alone.

2

Mount Flange with Screws

Secure the flange using 8×M5 and 2×M10 screws with spring washers. Torque M5 to 3–4 N·m and M10 to 12–15 N·m. Tighten in three stages: first the M10 bolts to 50% torque, then the M5 bolts to 50% torque, then all bolts to final torque in a cross pattern. Never torque M10 beyond 18 N·m — the AL6061 housing threads will strip. Use a calibrated torque wrench. After tightening, perform a 0.5 MPa air test before applying hydraulic pressure to verify flange integrity.

3

Secure Anti-Rotation

If the selected configuration includes an anti-rotation tab, secure it to a fixed bracket without applying axial or radial side load to the joint. Never let the stationary body rotate with the shaft. Leave appropriate clearance to prevent thermal expansion or alignment error from binding the shaft.

4

Connect Rated Flexible Supply Lines

Use flexible supply lines rated for the system medium and pressure, with a suitable safety margin above the 1 MPa maximum operating pressure. Prevent hose loads from transferring into the rotary joint. Install filtration appropriate to the selected medium and add pressure relief so system spikes cannot exceed the product rating. Label inlet and outlet ports clearly.

5

Run-In Procedure

Begin commissioning at low pressure and low speed without load. Check the flange and all port interfaces for leakage, then increase pressure and speed gradually while staying within the approved 1 MPa and 200 RPM limits. Stop the test if leakage, abnormal torque, vibration, or temperature rise is observed.

Maintenance Schedule

  • Inspection and replacement intervals must be established from the actual medium, pressure, speed, temperature, alignment, filtration, duty cycle, leakage trend, bearing condition, and recorded inspection results.

Common Mistakes

3 Mistakes That Destroy BP-2P-95-0001

Mistake 1: Torquing M10 bolts to M5 spec (or vice versa)

Incorrect fastener selection or torque can distort the flange and create a leak path even below the 1 MPa maximum pressure. Rule: Use the official 2D drawing and approved installation instructions to confirm the exact fastener pattern, tightening sequence, and torque before assembly.

Mistake 2: Operating without suitable filtration

Particles in air, water, coolant, or light oil can embed in the PTFE seal and score the sealing surface. Incorrect filtration or operation outside the specified limits can cause premature leakage. Rule: Select filtration according to the medium, contamination level, and duty cycle, and keep operating pressure at or below 1 MPa.

Mistake 3: Using it as 4 independent inlets instead of 2-in-4-out distribution

A machine designer needs 4 independent functions on a rotary table and assumes "4 outlets means 4 independent channels." BP-2P-95-0001 is a 2-in-4-out distributor: 2 inlets split to 4 outlets, not 4 independent inlets. Rule: Confirm the internal channel arrangement before connecting different pressure sources; specify a true 4-in-4-out design when four independent circuits are required.
